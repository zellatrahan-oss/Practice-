Membership

Rush Chi Beta Omega

Join Florida State’s most decorated business competition team, which is also a fraternity with a written position on mac-and-cheese bites.

Why join

What you actually get

Beyond the obvious, which is 213 phone numbers and a permanent seat at Thanksgiving-level roast sessions.

Skills you can prove

Career ready

Public speaking, financial modeling, case analysis, running a meeting that ends on time. These are the things interviewers ask about, and you will have specific, dated, verifiable answers.

A record that stands out

Internships & scholarships

Officer roles and competition placements do real work on an application, and the alumni board awards scholarships to brothers. Fourteen brothers landed summer internships last year. Eleven of them through alumni.

Travel and compete

Four conferences a year

Panama City, Orlando, Washington DC, and wherever Nationals lands. Business attire for three straight days, a van with a contested aux cord, and the best weekends of the year.

How to join

Four steps to initiation

  1. Rush

    Two weeks of open events at the start of each semester: info night, the résumé roast, a service project, and a cookout. Come to one, come to all four. Nothing is required, nothing is a test, and yes, there is always food.

    See the calendar →

  2. Pledge

    Ten weeks. A structured curriculum on the fraternity, business fundamentals, and chapter history, culminating in a 40-page chapter business plan your pledge class writes together and defends in front of the Executive Committee. There is no hazing here in any form. There is a lot of Excel.

  3. Pay dues

    $115 per semester, covering national, state, and local membership plus conference subsidies. Itemized below, because we are an accounting chapter and we cannot help ourselves.

  4. Initiate

    A ceremony, a pin, a handshake, and your name in the book. Then you are handed a folding chair, because the next pledge class arrives in fourteen weeks and the cycle is eternal.

Hardship fund

We keep a standing fund for brothers and prospective brothers for whom dues are a genuine obstacle. Requests go straight to the adviser, stay with the adviser, and have never once been denied. Cost is not why anyone misses out on this chapter.

FAQ

Common questions

Do I need to be a business major?

No, and roughly a third of the chapter is not. We have brothers in Engineering, Nursing, Communication, Criminology, and one in Philosophy who has won Public Speaking three years running and will not tell anyone how. Business knowledge is the thing we teach. Showing up is the thing you bring.

Do you haze?

No. Our national office, our adviser, Florida State, and the State of Florida are all extremely clear on this, and so are we — there is no hazing in this chapter in any form, and a brother who tries it is out the same night. What the pledge process actually consists of is a ten-week curriculum, a 40-page chapter business plan, and an unreasonable amount of folding-chair logistics. Some pledges claim they would prefer the other thing. They are joking. So are we, and only about the chairs.

How much time does this take?

One chapter meeting every other Wednesday, about fifty minutes. Add a practice session or two before each conference, plus whatever committee work you sign up for. Brothers who compete at Nationals are putting in real hours in February and March. Brothers who don't compete are looking at three or four hours a month.

What if dues are a problem for me?

Then email Dr. Whitfield-Brooks directly and it gets handled, confidentially, at the adviser level. No officer sees it and it is never discussed in chapter. We have a standing hardship fund for exactly this and we would much rather spend it than lose you.

Is there a GPA requirement?

2.75 to pledge, 2.5 to remain in good standing. If you fall below, you go on academic support rather than out the door: a study plan, a weekly check-in, and a brother assigned to you who has taken the class. Six brothers were on support last year. All six came off it.

Do I have to go to the Spring Formal?

No. It is optional, like everything social here. If you do come, you are bringing a date, because that is Article VII and it applies to everyone including the President, who was turned away for nine minutes in 2022.

Can I rush in the spring?

Yes. We take a pledge class each semester. Spring classes are smaller, which most brothers will tell you privately is the better experience.

Who do I actually contact?

Email our adviser at whitfield-brooks@fsu.edu, or any officer on the About page. We answer email within a day, which for a college organization is, frankly, a competitive advantage.
